The mistakes are quiet

Here is what a first release usually looks like. The song is finished, everyone is excited, it goes up within the week. It comes out. Some people stream it. Nothing obviously goes wrong.

Two years later: the producer thinks they own half the song and there's nothing in writing that says otherwise. The publishing royalties were never registered, so they've been sitting in a collection pot the whole time. The track was uploaded four days before release, so it was never pitched, so it never entered the algorithmic system that would have carried it. And the artist name is spelled two different ways across two profiles.

None of that felt like a mistake at the time, and that's exactly the problem. The errors are silent, and by the time they're visible they've already compounded.

Three things are true about this part of releasing music. Most of it is only expensive if you do it in the wrong order. A split sheet signed on the day costs nothing, while the same conversation two years later costs a friendship. Almost none of it is difficult either; it's just unfamiliar, and unfamiliar feels like hard. And the mistakes are quiet, so nothing breaks and you simply earn less than you should have, for years, without ever finding out why.

Questions

Is this just the free stuff on the site?

No. The site has a split sheet calculator and two articles. This is the full process, in order, with the four other templates and the parts that don't work as free articles: the metadata checks, the timeline generator, the pitch example.

Do I need to install anything?

No. The templates open in any browser and work offline. Spreadsheet and document versions are included if you'd rather work that way.

Is this US-only?

No. Publishing registration differs by country and the guide names the organisations for the US, UK, Australia and New Zealand. Everything else, meaning ownership, distribution, metadata, timing and tracking, works anywhere.

I've already released music. Is it too late?

For that release, some of it. For the catalogue, mostly not, because publishing registration is retroactive and Section 7 covers it. It's tedious and partial, and it's still money.

Will it go out of date?

Parts of it will; platform terms change constantly. That's why every figure is dated and why updates are included permanently. When something changes, the file changes.

Thirty days, no questions, no form. If you read it and it didn't tell you anything you didn't already know, that's a refund, and it's genuinely fine to ask.
