The rule

Publishing what you got wrong is the cheapest credibility available, and almost nobody does it. Why every piece ends with the receipts

Every piece closes with the same three-part block: what holds up, what's shaky, and what didn't survive checking. That last section is the point. It's where an earlier draft's four wrong distributor figures live, and where I record that I wrongly said Ace Hood had stopped releasing music.

When a number here turns out to be wrong, the page changes and says so, with the date.

Why this exists

Going cold turkey means quitting the thing everyone tells you that you can't quit. Cool turkey is doing it without the misery, and without pretending it's easy. For artists that means the label path and the guru advice. For everyone else it means the feed deciding what you hear.

There's an enormous amount of music media covering who is beefing with whom, and almost none covering the fact that your distributor quietly takes 20% of your TikTok money, or that the research on whether lyrics even matter says something stranger than either side of that argument believes. Both of those are worth someone's time.
